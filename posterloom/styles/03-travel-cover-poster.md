# Travel Cover Poster

## Style intent
Treat the place itself as the icon. Preserve destination-defining roofs, waterways, paths, skyline, mountain profile, street rhythm, and real cultural details. Never fabricate a place name.

## Best for
- travel
- landscape
- architecture
- old towns
- waterside scenes
- mountains
- streets

## Reject when
- the task depends on unverified location facts
- the source is a product close-up
- the user wants full abstraction

## Style DNA
**Subject / identity behavior**  
Treat the place itself as the icon. Preserve destination-defining roofs, waterways, paths, skyline, mountain profile, street rhythm, and real cultural details. Never fabricate a place name.

**Composition strategy**  
Use real spatial cues—paths, bridges, water edges, roof lines, vegetation, sky, streets, mountain contours—to lead toward one destination anchor.

**Lighting language**  
Maintain plausible time of day, weather, sun direction, haze, and atmospheric perspective.

**Color logic**  
Refine the source into roughly 3–5 dominant travel-editorial tones without replacing local color.

**Material / texture language**  
Retain weathered walls, tile, timber, water, stone, vegetation, and air quality.

**Typography system**  
Small editorial title. Use a verified place name only if supplied by the source/user; otherwise use a mood-based title.

**Realism level**  
high

**Transformation strength**  
medium

## Execution contract
1. Treat the uploaded source photograph as the single factual and visual anchor.
2. Protect the scene-analysis identity anchors before stylization.
3. Produce one finished poster, not a collage, contact sheet, moodboard, scrapbook, album, or multi-panel layout.
4. Preserve source truth: do not invent people, brands, dates, landmarks, signatures, seals, costumes, or factual claims.
5. Apply this style through its actual visual mechanism, not a generic preset.
6. Keep typography subordinate to the image, normally no more than 5–10% of the visual area.
7. Use the source-derived palette unless the style contract explicitly permits a controlled reinterpretation.
8. Stop stylization before it damages identity, perspective, material plausibility, or cultural accuracy.

## Style-specific prompt block
Transform the single source photograph using the **Travel Cover Poster** visual system. Treat the photograph as the factual anchor and preserve all identity anchors before stylization.

Mechanism: Treat the place itself as the icon. Preserve destination-defining roofs, waterways, paths, skyline, mountain profile, street rhythm, and real cultural details. Never fabricate a place name.

Composition: Use real spatial cues—paths, bridges, water edges, roof lines, vegetation, sky, streets, mountain contours—to lead toward one destination anchor.

Lighting: Maintain plausible time of day, weather, sun direction, haze, and atmospheric perspective.

Color: Refine the source into roughly 3–5 dominant travel-editorial tones without replacing local color.

Material and texture: Retain weathered walls, tile, timber, water, stone, vegetation, and air quality.

Typography: Small editorial title. Use a verified place name only if supplied by the source/user; otherwise use a mood-based title.

The result must be materially different from a one-click filter. Every stylized decision should be traceable either to the source image or to the defined Travel Cover Poster mechanism. Preserve subject count, perspective, culturally meaningful details, and source-specific geometry. Do not invent facts, people, landmarks, brands, dates, signatures, or unreadable text. Stop the transformation before source identity is damaged.

## Style-specific negative prompt
No fake landmark, postcard border, travel-agency logo, invented place name, heavy vintage filter, oversaturated tourism sky, giant destination lettering, collage layout, or motivational slogan.

## Variation knobs
- destination anchor 0.35–0.65
- sky/water breathing room medium/high
- palette 3–5 tones
- clarity natural/polished
- title mood-based/verified-place

## Hybrid behavior
Direct styles may enter `Controlled Hybrid` only if their pair appears in `data/compatibility.json`. The primary keeps subject identity, composition, lighting, palette, and realism. The secondary controls one declared subsystem only.

## Common failure modes
- Applying the same texture or effect uniformly to every pixel.
- Replacing source-specific structure with generic AI imagery.
- Oversizing typography until text becomes the subject.
- Inventing content to make a style stereotype more obvious.
- Ignoring material differences between surfaces.
- Turning batch consistency into forced visual sameness.

## Self-check
- Is the source still immediately recognizable?
- Can major stylized elements be traced to the source or this style's mechanism?
- Is the composition materially different from a simple filter?
- Is text small, legible, and outside identity-critical regions?
- Are there invented facts, extra subjects, malformed structures, or fake characters?
- Does the output read as `Travel Cover Poster` specifically rather than generic “AI art”?

## Acceptance criteria
Use `references/visual-benchmark.md`. A production image should normally score at least 17/20 across identity, composition, lighting, palette, material behavior, edges, typography, source specificity, style specificity, and anti-slop quality.
