# Pearl Saint Beauty

## Style intent
Apply the four-stage cocoa-and-pearl protocol below to the source face. The bone structure underneath does not change — the makeup sits on fixed geometry and never replaces it. Photograph it as a vintage cold-white studio beauty portrait: one soft key, pale seamless backdrop, shallow depth of field, so the pearls read as the only hard specular objects in a soft warm-toned face.

## Best for
- beauty and makeup portrait
- editorial fashion beauty shot
- stylised studio portrait with a cosmetic concept
- social-media portrait with a named makeup look

## Reject when
- no human subject is present
- the user wants the face left untouched
- the brief is documentary reportage rather than a constructed look
- the source is already heavy graphic art

## Style DNA
**Subject / identity behavior**  
Apply the four-stage cocoa-and-pearl protocol below to the source face. The bone structure underneath does not change — the makeup sits on fixed geometry and never replaces it. Photograph it as a vintage cold-white studio beauty portrait: one soft key, pale seamless backdrop, shallow depth of field, so the pearls read as the only hard specular objects in a soft warm-toned face.

**Composition strategy**  
Vertical studio beauty portrait, head and shoulders. The face on the upper third with clean headroom; the pearls on the under-eye and cheekbone so they read within the face. Keep the whole face inside the frame with margin around the hairline and chin, and keep the pearls clear of the pupil and the lip.

**Lighting language**  
One soft key above left, broad wrap over the cheekbone. Cold white seamless backdrop one stop above the skin. The champagne highlight is the brightest point on the face.

**Color logic**  
Warm cocoa-and-pearl palette: warm beige and taupe skin, champagne-gold highlight, taupe and dusty rose eyes, matte apricot blush, nude-pink lip, cool ivory pearls. The pearls are the only cool element in an otherwise warm frame.

**Material / texture language**  
Skin rendered with visible fine texture and a thin semi-matte base — the base stays thin enough that the champagne highlight sits on it without patchiness. Highlight reads as a broad soft metallic sheen, not glitter specks. Pearls read as hard round nacreous beads with a small bright specular point and a soft reflected highlight, slightly irregular in size, loosely clustered rather than in rows. Lashes read as separated clumps. Lip reads as a thin sheer tint with the lip texture still visible. No plastic smoothing, no beauty-filter skin, no airbrushing.

**Typography system**  
Optional small editorial serif, usually 3-6% of poster height, in the upper margin above the head or the lower corner, never crossing the face, the pearls or the hands.

**Realism level**  
high

**Transformation strength**  
medium

## Execution contract
1. Treat the uploaded source photograph as the single factual and visual anchor.
2. Protect the scene-analysis identity anchors before stylization.
3. Produce one finished poster, not a collage, contact sheet, moodboard, scrapbook, album, or multi-panel layout.
4. Preserve source truth: do not invent people, brands, dates, landmarks, signatures, seals, costumes, or factual claims.
5. Apply this style through its actual visual mechanism, not a generic preset.
6. Keep typography subordinate to the image, normally no more than 5–10% of the visual area.
7. Use the source-derived palette unless the style contract explicitly permits a controlled reinterpretation.
8. Stop stylization before it damages identity, perspective, material plausibility, or cultural accuracy.

## Style-specific prompt block
Transform the source photograph using the **Pearl Saint Beauty** visual system. The photograph is the factual anchor and the face is FIXED GEOMETRY. The makeup is the requested content and MUST be applied to that exact face.

Makeup protocol, in order: (1) thin semi-matte base, skin texture still visible, localised concealer only; (2) a soft warm champagne sheen across the cheekbone, inner eye, brow bone and nose tip; (3) taupe shadow over the lid, dusty rose at the outer eye, thin deep-brown liner, lashes in separated clumps; (4) matte apricot blush swept from under-eye to mid-face, nude-pink tint on the lips with the lip texture showing through.

Pearls: 8 to 12 solid opaque ivory beads set into the skin, each a smooth round matte-nacre sphere, across the under-eye, outer eye and upper cheekbone. Uneven sizes, loose clusters, never a row, never over the pupil or the lip. Cool ivory with a faint blue-grey nacre sheen — the only hard specular objects in a soft warm-toned face.

Setting: vintage cold-white studio portrait. One large soft key slightly above and to the left, broad wrap across the cheekbone, soft falloff into the jaw, pale seamless backdrop one stop above the skin, shallow depth of field, vertical frame.

Colour: warm cocoa-and-pearl palette — warm beige and taupe base, champagne-gold highlight, taupe and dusty rose eyes, matte apricot blush, nude-pink lip, cool ivory pearls. Warm-neutral overall; the pearls are the only cool element.

Typography: optional small editorial serif in the upper margin or lower corner, never across the face or the pearls.

## Style-specific negative prompt
no rows of pearls, no pearls over the pupil or lip, no silver or blue-white highlight, no heavy foundation mask, no plastic or airbrushed skin, no changed face shape or width, no changed jawline or chin, no altered eye shape, eye size, or eye spacing, no nose width or bridge change, no lip or mouth width change, no age shift or de-aging, no face slimming, no eye enlargement, no new or different person, no model-default or idealized face, no celebrity lookalike.

## Common failure modes
- Applying the same texture or effect uniformly to every pixel.
- Replacing source-specific structure with generic AI imagery.
- Oversizing typography until text becomes the subject.
- Inventing content to make a style stereotype more obvious.
- Ignoring material differences between surfaces.
- Turning batch consistency into forced visual sameness.

## Self-check
- Is the source still immediately recognizable?
- Can major stylized elements be traced to the source or this style's mechanism?
- Is the composition materially different from a simple filter?
- Is text small, legible, and outside identity-critical regions?
- Are there invented facts, extra subjects, malformed structures, or fake characters?
- Does the output read as this style specifically rather than generic “AI art”?
