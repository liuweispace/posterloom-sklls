# Riso Poster

## Style intent
Preserve subject contour and essential internal detail, then reinterpret the image as intentional print separations.

## Best for
- architecture
- still life
- people
- plants
- street
- culture
- graphic scenes

## Reject when
- high-gloss realism is required
- many essential colors cannot be reduced
- photoreal material rendering is the priority

## Style DNA
**Subject / identity behavior**  
Preserve subject contour and essential internal detail, then reinterpret the image as intentional print separations.

**Composition strategy**  
Use large graphic masses with selective fine-line detail around the focal subject.

**Lighting language**  
Translate illumination into spot-color density and overprint relationships.

**Color logic**  
Use 2–4 source-derived riso-like spot inks with meaningful overprint.

**Material / texture language**  
Coarse attractive grain, visible paper fibers, slight registration drift, uneven ink density, and softly printed edges.

**Typography system**  
Small independent-publishing sans or serif with generous spacing.

**Realism level**  
low-medium

**Transformation strength**  
high

## Execution contract
1. Treat the uploaded source photograph as the single factual and visual anchor.
2. Protect the scene-analysis identity anchors before stylization.
3. Produce one finished poster, not a collage, contact sheet, moodboard, scrapbook, album, or multi-panel layout.
4. Preserve source truth: do not invent people, brands, dates, landmarks, signatures, seals, costumes, or factual claims.
5. Apply this style through its actual visual mechanism, not a generic preset.
6. Keep typography subordinate to the image, normally no more than 5–10% of the visual area.
7. Use the source-derived palette unless the style contract explicitly permits a controlled reinterpretation.
8. Stop stylization before it damages identity, perspective, material plausibility, or cultural accuracy.

## Style-specific prompt block
Transform the single source photograph using the **Riso Poster** visual system. Treat the photograph as the factual anchor and preserve all identity anchors before stylization.

Mechanism: Preserve subject contour and essential internal detail, then reinterpret the image as intentional print separations.

Composition: Use large graphic masses with selective fine-line detail around the focal subject.

Lighting: Translate illumination into spot-color density and overprint relationships.

Color: Use 2–4 source-derived riso-like spot inks with meaningful overprint.

Material and texture: Coarse attractive grain, visible paper fibers, slight registration drift, uneven ink density, and softly printed edges.

Typography: Small independent-publishing sans or serif with generous spacing.

The result must be materially different from a one-click filter. Every stylized decision should be traceable either to the source image or to the defined Riso Poster mechanism. Preserve subject count, perspective, culturally meaningful details, and source-specific geometry. Do not invent facts, people, landmarks, brands, dates, signatures, or unreadable text. Stop the transformation before source identity is damaged.

## Style-specific negative prompt
No glossy gradients, full-color photorealism, random noise, fake old-paper stains, chaotic zine collage, too many ink colors, sticker graphics, or cartoon conversion.

## Variation knobs
- ink count 2–4
- overprint low/medium
- registration drift tiny/small
- paper grain medium
- line detail focal-only

## Hybrid behavior
Direct styles may enter `Controlled Hybrid` only if their pair appears in `data/compatibility.json`. The primary keeps subject identity, composition, lighting, palette, and realism. The secondary controls one declared subsystem only.

## Common failure modes
- Applying the same texture or effect uniformly to every pixel.
- Replacing source-specific structure with generic AI imagery.
- Oversizing typography until text becomes the subject.
- Inventing content to make a style stereotype more obvious.
- Ignoring material differences between surfaces.
- Turning batch consistency into forced visual sameness.

## Self-check
- Is the source still immediately recognizable?
- Can major stylized elements be traced to the source or this style's mechanism?
- Is the composition materially different from a simple filter?
- Is text small, legible, and outside identity-critical regions?
- Are there invented facts, extra subjects, malformed structures, or fake characters?
- Does the output read as `Riso Poster` specifically rather than generic “AI art”?

## Acceptance criteria
Use `references/visual-benchmark.md`. A production image should normally score at least 17/20 across identity, composition, lighting, palette, material behavior, edges, typography, source specificity, style specificity, and anti-slop quality.
