# Scene Distillation Zine

## Style intent
Treat the photo as semantic evidence and creative stimulus, never as a visual layer. The output contains original illustration, paper and typography only - no photographic pixels, no tracing, no photorealistic regions. The photo is a reference for meaning, not a layer to keep.

## Best for
- authored abstract reinterpretations
- emotional or conceptual poster series
- editorial pages that should not read as photographs
- abstract art posters from a personal photo
- work where the source is stimulus, not content

## Reject when
- the user wants the photograph preserved or visible in the output
- the user needs the source person recognizable
- the request is a straightforward photo-to-poster conversion
- the poster must carry commercial product imagery

## Style DNA
**Subject / identity behavior**  
Treat the photo as semantic evidence and creative stimulus, never as a visual layer. The output contains original illustration, paper and typography only - no photographic pixels, no tracing, no photorealistic regions. The photo is a reference for meaning, not a layer to keep.

**Composition strategy**  
Preserve the source orientation as part of its spatial logic: vertical 3:5 for a portrait source, horizontal 5:3 for a landscape source, vertical 3:5 when square or ambiguous. Recompose freely inside that canvas rather than retaining the photographic framing. Start from 68-85% quiet paper and one active illustration cluster of roughly 12-32% of the canvas, then correct by actual visual weight. Choose ONE composition family: asymmetric island, torn window, directional drift, rhythmic circulation, staggered fragments, vertical tension, or auxiliary constellation. Never center by habit or split the canvas evenly.

**Lighting language**  
No lighting at all in the photographic sense. Flat printed colour on paper. The image is made of ink, paper and type, so there is no light source, no depth of field and no cast shadow. Depth comes from overlapping paper forms and edge treatment only.

**Color logic**  
Paper and most illustration inks stay subdued; ONE unmistakable high-chroma hue carries the work. Give it a single role - focal pin, counterweight, bridge, directional cue, or rhythmic beat - and keep the total high-chroma area around 0.8-3% of the poster, or 10-30% of the active cluster. State the eye path through it. Do not add a bright shape without compositional reason.

**Material / texture language**  
Torn fiber, dry ink, grain, cut paper and broken contour carry meaning, not surface decoration. Paper is warm cream with visible fiber, scan grain and flat print texture. Forms are flat masses with broken edges, never realistic shading, never polished vector, never cute cartoon.

**Typography system**  
Fully free authorial material. Wording, language, amount, placement, type voices, scale, direction and legibility all follow expression rather than preset. Text may be caption, countervoice, title, interruption, rhythm, surface texture, or the primary subject. It may be tiny, oversized, cropped, scattered, stacked, rotated, obscured or materially mixed with the illustration. The only rule: it must deepen the proposition, tension, metaphor or interpretive opening rather than caption what is visible.

**Realism level**  
low

**Transformation strength**  
high

## Execution contract
1. Treat the uploaded source photograph as the single factual and visual anchor.
2. Protect the scene-analysis identity anchors before stylization.
3. Produce one finished poster, not a collage, contact sheet, moodboard, scrapbook, album, or multi-panel layout.
4. Preserve source truth: do not invent people, brands, dates, landmarks, signatures, seals, costumes, or factual claims.
5. Apply this style through its actual visual mechanism, not a generic preset.
6. Keep typography subordinate to the image, normally no more than 5–10% of the visual area.
7. Use the source-derived palette unless the style contract explicitly permits a controlled reinterpretation.
8. Stop stylization before it damages identity, perspective, material plausibility, or cultural accuracy.

## Style-specific prompt block
Transform the single source photograph using the **Scene Distillation Zine** visual system. The photo is semantic evidence and creative stimulus only.


NO PHOTOGRAPHY. Do not reproduce, embed, crop, collage, trace, or retain photographic pixels or photorealistic regions from the reference. The final image must contain original illustration, paper and typography only.

Reading: identify the semantic nucleus (the smallest subject, relationship or event that gives the source meaning), one core subject or at most two inseparable ones, one to three supporting elements, the dominant gesture, one spatial cue worth preserving, the visual-weight map, the native palette, the material and weather, the emotional residue (the feeling that remains after factual description is removed), the discard list, and the forms that can be enlarged, merged, fragmented or turned into negative space. Preserve only two to four source anchors. Do not preserve the original composition.

Expression: build the artwork through source fact → emotional residue → expressive proposition → central tension → visual metaphor → formal embodiment → interpretive opening. Write one specific relational proposition the artwork asks the viewer to feel, notice or reconsider - not a generic mood label. Choose ONE central tension that already exists or can credibly emerge: intimacy/distance, shelter/confinement, movement/stillness, smallness/vastness, warmth/coldness, memory/disappearance, order/growth, visibility/concealment, or permanence/fragility. Turn ONE source-derived object or relationship into the central metaphor, letting it shift function. Leave one relationship deliberately unresolved so the viewer can enter the work without a single fixed reading. Remove any element whose only function is to make the image look artistic.

Canvas and composition: preserve the source orientation as spatial logic - vertical 3:5 for a portrait source, horizontal 5:3 for a landscape source, vertical 3:5 when square or ambiguous. Recompose freely inside that canvas. Begin from 68-85% quiet paper and one active illustration cluster of about 12-32%, then correct by actual visual weight rather than by area. Choose one composition family - asymmetric island, torn window, directional drift, rhythmic circulation, staggered fragments, vertical tension, or auxiliary constellation - and use scale, interval, enclosure, direction and quiet space to embody the proposition. Use auxiliary constellation only when a source-derived supporting motif can carry rhythm and meaning. Maintain figure-ground clarity, asymmetric balance, dominant-subordinate hierarchy, optical rather than mechanical centering, and an eye path with a clear entry, focal encounter, movement and quiet exit.

Abstraction and grammar: editorial abstraction. Remove roughly 65-90% of descriptive detail. Replace realistic rendering with simplified masses, broken contours, paper fragments, sparse marks or print fields. Choose ONE primary illustration grammar - cut-paper mass, dry-print silhouette, broken contour, rhythm field, fragment stack, or orbit/drift - and at most one supporting grammar.

Edge: choose ONE primary edge treatment by its semantic function - torn-fiber, layered grayscale, stippled dissolution, irregular mark, or a quiet natural isolated contour with no visible transition device. Align the transition with a source-derived horizon, gesture, path, pressure or directional break. Use positive and negative shapes with internal paper gaps, let zero to two forms cross the boundary when continuation helps, and keep the depth shallow and tactile.

Color: keep the paper and most illustration inks subdued, then use exactly one unmistakable high-chroma hue as chromatic structure rather than decoration. Give the accent one role - focal pin, counterweight, bridge between fragments, directional cue, or rhythmic beat - and keep the total high-chroma area around 0.8-3% of the poster, or 10-30% of the active cluster. Use one main accent plus one or two smaller echoes kept together below 25% of the total accent area. If the source contains a meaningful repeatable motif, you may disperse several instances of it in that single hue with unequal scale and interval, leaving some intervals open.

Typography: fully free authorial material. Wording, language, amount, type voices, scale, direction, hierarchy, placement and legibility all follow expression rather than a preset. The text may be caption, countervoice, title, interruption, rhythm, surface texture, or the image's primary subject, and it may be tiny, oversized, cropped, scattered, stacked, rotated, obscured or materially mixed with the illustration. The only rule: it must deepen the proposition, tension, metaphor or interpretive opening rather than caption what is visible.

## Hybrid behavior
Direct styles may enter `Controlled Hybrid` only if their pair appears in `data/compatibility.json`. The primary keeps subject identity, composition, lighting, palette, and realism. The secondary controls one declared subsystem only.

## Common failure modes
- Applying the same texture or effect uniformly to every pixel.
- Replacing source-specific structure with generic AI imagery.
- Oversizing typography until text becomes the subject.
- Inventing content to make a style stereotype more obvious.
- Ignoring material differences between surfaces.
- Turning batch consistency into forced visual sameness.

## Self-check
- Is the source still immediately recognizable?
- Can major stylized elements be traced to the source or this style's mechanism?
- Is the composition materially different from a simple filter?
- Is text small, legible, and outside identity-critical regions?
- Are there invented facts, extra subjects, malformed structures, or fake characters?
- Does the output read as this style specifically rather than generic “AI art”?

## Acceptance criteria
Use `references/visual-benchmark.md`. A production image should normally score at least 17/20 across identity, composition, lighting, palette, material behavior, edges, typography, source specificity, style specificity, and anti-slop quality.
