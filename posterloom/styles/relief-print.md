# Relief Print

## Style intent
Lock silhouette, anatomy, pose, object count, structural geometry, and crucial contours. Think in carved positive/negative mass.

## Best for
- architecture
- sculpture
- industrial
- vehicles
- trees
- strong portraits
- dramatic shadows

## Reject when
- soft mist is the core visual quality
- beauty skin rendering is important
- the scene depends on many colors

## Style DNA
**Subject / identity behavior**  
Lock silhouette, anatomy, pose, object count, structural geometry, and crucial contours. Think in carved positive/negative mass.

**Composition strategy**  
Map positive ink masses and negative cut-away paper before adding texture. Use carving density to describe form.

**Lighting language**  
Convert light into a carved value hierarchy rather than smooth shading.

**Color logic**  
Mostly black, charcoal, warm paper, with at most one restrained source-derived accent.

**Material / texture language**  
Directional gouge marks, uneven carved edges, ink-load variation, paper tooth, and purposeful cut rhythm.

**Typography system**  
Small geometric contemporary type, never pseudo-historical woodcut lettering.

**Realism level**  
low-medium

**Transformation strength**  
high

## Execution contract
1. Treat the uploaded source photograph as the single factual and visual anchor.
2. Protect the scene-analysis identity anchors before stylization.
3. Produce one finished poster, not a collage, contact sheet, moodboard, scrapbook, album, or multi-panel layout.
4. Preserve source truth: do not invent people, brands, dates, landmarks, signatures, seals, costumes, or factual claims.
5. Apply this style through its actual visual mechanism, not a generic preset.
6. Keep typography subordinate to the image, normally no more than 5–10% of the visual area.
7. Use the source-derived palette unless the style contract explicitly permits a controlled reinterpretation.
8. Stop stylization before it damages identity, perspective, material plausibility, or cultural accuracy.

## Style-specific prompt block
Transform the single source photograph using the **Relief Print** visual system. Treat the photograph as the factual anchor and preserve all identity anchors before stylization.

Mechanism: Lock silhouette, anatomy, pose, object count, structural geometry, and crucial contours. Think in carved positive/negative mass.

Composition: Map positive ink masses and negative cut-away paper before adding texture. Use carving density to describe form.

Lighting: Convert light into a carved value hierarchy rather than smooth shading.

Color: Mostly black, charcoal, warm paper, with at most one restrained source-derived accent.

Material and texture: Directional gouge marks, uneven carved edges, ink-load variation, paper tooth, and purposeful cut rhythm.

The result must be materially different from a one-click filter. Every stylized decision should be traceable either to the source image or to the defined Relief Print mechanism. Preserve subject count, perspective, culturally meaningful details, and source-specific geometry. Do not invent facts, people, landmarks, brands, dates, signatures, or unreadable text. Stop the transformation before source identity is damaged.

## Style-specific negative prompt
No uniform Photoshop edge filter, comic outline, horror-grunge scratches, random distress, smooth gradient shading, many bright colors, or loss of body/architecture geometry.

## Variation knobs
- carving density by zone
- accent color 0–1
- gouge direction structural
- ink mass medium/high
- paper show-through medium

## Hybrid behavior
Direct styles may enter `Controlled Hybrid` only if their pair appears in `data/compatibility.json`. The primary keeps subject identity, composition, lighting, palette, and realism. The secondary controls one declared subsystem only.

## Common failure modes
- Applying the same texture or effect uniformly to every pixel.
- Replacing source-specific structure with generic AI imagery.
- Oversizing typography until text becomes the subject.
- Inventing content to make a style stereotype more obvious.
- Ignoring material differences between surfaces.
- Turning batch consistency into forced visual sameness.

## Self-check
- Is the source still immediately recognizable?
- Can major stylized elements be traced to the source or this style's mechanism?
- Is the composition materially different from a simple filter?
- Is text small, legible, and outside identity-critical regions?
- Are there invented facts, extra subjects, malformed structures, or fake characters?
- Does the output read as `Relief Print` specifically rather than generic “AI art”?

## Acceptance criteria
Use `references/visual-benchmark.md`. A production image should normally score at least 17/20 across identity, composition, lighting, palette, material behavior, edges, typography, source specificity, style specificity, and anti-slop quality.
