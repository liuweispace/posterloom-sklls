# Cinematic Editorial

## Style intent
Preserve photographic credibility. Lock face/posture, architecture silhouette, object proportions, real signage placement, and source-specific anchors. Art direction changes hierarchy, not factual identity.

## Best for
- travel
- architecture
- cultural spaces
- street
- lifestyle
- night environments

## Reject when
- source is already graphic art
- full abstraction is the explicit goal
- identity cannot be established

## Style DNA
**Subject / identity behavior**  
Preserve photographic credibility. Lock face/posture, architecture silhouette, object proportions, real signage placement, and source-specific anchors. Art direction changes hierarchy, not factual identity.

**Composition strategy**  
Use cinematic crop, controlled negative space, one clear focal path, believable depth, and selective background simplification. Prefer crop/scale/spacing before content invention.

**Lighting language**  
Preserve light direction and motivation. Refine practical lights, dusk, side light, window glow, and shadow separation; unsupported key lights are forbidden.

**Color logic**  
Source-derived filmic grading: gentle highlight rolloff, deep readable blacks, restrained saturation, subtle warm/cool separation.

**Material / texture language**  
Keep stone, wood, skin, ceramic, glass, foliage, water, plaster, metal, and fabric physically believable; grain may unify but not erase surface identity.

**Typography system**  
Small contemporary serif, grotesk, or condensed sans. Usually 2–6% of poster height, placed only in a genuine text-safe zone.

**Realism level**  
high

**Transformation strength**  
medium

## Execution contract
1. Treat the uploaded source photograph as the single factual and visual anchor.
2. Protect the scene-analysis identity anchors before stylization.
3. Produce one finished poster, not a collage, contact sheet, moodboard, scrapbook, album, or multi-panel layout.
4. Preserve source truth: do not invent people, brands, dates, landmarks, signatures, seals, costumes, or factual claims.
5. Apply this style through its actual visual mechanism, not a generic preset.
6. Keep typography subordinate to the image, normally no more than 5–10% of the visual area.
7. Use the source-derived palette unless the style contract explicitly permits a controlled reinterpretation.
8. Stop stylization before it damages identity, perspective, material plausibility, or cultural accuracy.

## Style-specific prompt block
Transform the single source photograph using the **Cinematic Editorial** visual system. Treat the photograph as the factual anchor and preserve all identity anchors before stylization.

Mechanism: Preserve photographic credibility. Lock face/posture, architecture silhouette, object proportions, real signage placement, and source-specific anchors. Art direction changes hierarchy, not factual identity.

Composition: Use cinematic crop, controlled negative space, one clear focal path, believable depth, and selective background simplification. Prefer crop/scale/spacing before content invention.

Lighting: Preserve light direction and motivation. Refine practical lights, dusk, side light, window glow, and shadow separation; unsupported key lights are forbidden.

Color: Source-derived filmic grading: gentle highlight rolloff, deep readable blacks, restrained saturation, subtle warm/cool separation.

Material and texture: Keep stone, wood, skin, ceramic, glass, foliage, water, plaster, metal, and fabric physically believable; grain may unify but not erase surface identity.

Typography: Small contemporary serif, grotesk, or condensed sans. Usually 2–6% of poster height, placed only in a genuine text-safe zone.

The result must be materially different from a one-click filter. Every stylized decision should be traceable either to the source image or to the defined Cinematic Editorial mechanism. Preserve subject count, perspective, culturally meaningful details, and source-specific geometry. Do not invent facts, people, landmarks, brands, dates, signatures, or unreadable text. Stop the transformation before source identity is damaged.

## Style-specific negative prompt
No fantasy reconstruction, cyberpunk conversion, giant headline, fake film border, arbitrary haze, random particles, excessive bloom, duplicated objects, impossible perspective, plastic skin, or generic orange-teal grading.

## Variation knobs
- subject scale 0.40–0.72
- negative space medium/high
- grain fine/medium
- contrast soft/medium
- type serif/grotesk/condensed

## Hybrid behavior
Direct styles may enter `Controlled Hybrid` only if their pair appears in `data/compatibility.json`. The primary keeps subject identity, composition, lighting, palette, and realism. The secondary controls one declared subsystem only.

## Common failure modes
- Applying the same texture or effect uniformly to every pixel.
- Replacing source-specific structure with generic AI imagery.
- Oversizing typography until text becomes the subject.
- Inventing content to make a style stereotype more obvious.
- Ignoring material differences between surfaces.
- Turning batch consistency into forced visual sameness.

## Self-check
- Is the source still immediately recognizable?
- Can major stylized elements be traced to the source or this style's mechanism?
- Is the composition materially different from a simple filter?
- Is text small, legible, and outside identity-critical regions?
- Are there invented facts, extra subjects, malformed structures, or fake characters?
- Does the output read as `Cinematic Editorial` specifically rather than generic “AI art”?

## Acceptance criteria
Use `references/visual-benchmark.md`. A production image should normally score at least 17/20 across identity, composition, lighting, palette, material behavior, edges, typography, source specificity, style specificity, and anti-slop quality.
