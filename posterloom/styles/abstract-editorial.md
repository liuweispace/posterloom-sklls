# Abstract Editorial

## Style intent
Keep one or two recognizable identity anchors while allowing secondary information to dissolve into source-derived abstraction.

## Best for
- architecture
- shadow
- reflection
- pattern
- urban
- still life
- strong color fields

## Reject when
- literal identity is mandatory
- documentary context must remain complete
- the source has no strong structural relationship

## Style DNA
**Subject / identity behavior**  
Keep one or two recognizable identity anchors while allowing secondary information to dissolve into source-derived abstraction.

**Composition strategy**  
Reconstruct from real geometric/organic masses, directional lines, repetitions, voids, shadows, and color fields from the source.

**Lighting language**  
Translate illumination into value fields and transparent overlays rather than photographic effects.

**Color logic**  
Use a simplified source palette; transparent overlays are allowed only when they derive from real source relationships.

**Material / texture language**  
Flat color, translucent layers, subtle paper/print texture, and optional restrained photographic fragments.

**Typography system**  
Clean minimal typography used as a compositional element, not a headline.

**Realism level**  
low-medium

**Transformation strength**  
high

## Execution contract
1. Treat the uploaded source photograph as the single factual and visual anchor.
2. Protect the scene-analysis identity anchors before stylization.
3. Produce one finished poster, not a collage, contact sheet, moodboard, scrapbook, album, or multi-panel layout.
4. Preserve source truth: do not invent people, brands, dates, landmarks, signatures, seals, costumes, or factual claims.
5. Apply this style through its actual visual mechanism, not a generic preset.
6. Keep typography subordinate to the image, normally no more than 5–10% of the visual area.
7. Use the source-derived palette unless the style contract explicitly permits a controlled reinterpretation.
8. Stop stylization before it damages identity, perspective, material plausibility, or cultural accuracy.

## Style-specific prompt block
Transform the single source photograph using the **Abstract Editorial** visual system. Treat the photograph as the factual anchor and preserve all identity anchors before stylization.

Mechanism: Keep one or two recognizable identity anchors while allowing secondary information to dissolve into source-derived abstraction.

Composition: Reconstruct from real geometric/organic masses, directional lines, repetitions, voids, shadows, and color fields from the source.

Lighting: Translate illumination into value fields and transparent overlays rather than photographic effects.

Color: Use a simplified source palette; transparent overlays are allowed only when they derive from real source relationships.

Material and texture: Flat color, translucent layers, subtle paper/print texture, and optional restrained photographic fragments.

Typography: Clean minimal typography used as a compositional element, not a headline.

The result must be materially different from a one-click filter. Every stylized decision should be traceable either to the source image or to the defined Abstract Editorial mechanism. Preserve subject count, perspective, culturally meaningful details, and source-specific geometry. Do not invent facts, people, landmarks, brands, dates, signatures, or unreadable text. Stop the transformation before source identity is damaged.

## Style-specific negative prompt
No random circles/triangles, rainbow palette, meaningless Bauhaus imitation, chaotic collage, decorative gradient, total loss of source, or large slogan.

## Variation knobs
- identity anchors 1–2
- photo fragment 0–20%
- overlay opacity 15–45%
- palette 3–6
- negative space high

## Hybrid behavior
Direct styles may enter `Controlled Hybrid` only if their pair appears in `data/compatibility.json`. The primary keeps subject identity, composition, lighting, palette, and realism. The secondary controls one declared subsystem only.

## Common failure modes
- Applying the same texture or effect uniformly to every pixel.
- Replacing source-specific structure with generic AI imagery.
- Oversizing typography until text becomes the subject.
- Inventing content to make a style stereotype more obvious.
- Ignoring material differences between surfaces.
- Turning batch consistency into forced visual sameness.

## Self-check
- Is the source still immediately recognizable?
- Can major stylized elements be traced to the source or this style's mechanism?
- Is the composition materially different from a simple filter?
- Is text small, legible, and outside identity-critical regions?
- Are there invented facts, extra subjects, malformed structures, or fake characters?
- Does the output read as `Abstract Editorial` specifically rather than generic “AI art”?

## Acceptance criteria
Use `references/visual-benchmark.md`. A production image should normally score at least 17/20 across identity, composition, lighting, palette, material behavior, edges, typography, source specificity, style specificity, and anti-slop quality.
