# Gathered Scenes Zine

## Style intent
Keep the photograph truthful as the factual anchor, then let a larger abstract illustration field reinterpret selected source elements. The two materials meet at a visible hand-torn fibrous edge. The photograph provides the facts; the illustration decides how they remain.

## Best for
- travel and place photographs
- landscape and architecture with strong geometry
- botanical and organic detail
- quiet editorial poster pages
- tactile paper-collage posters

## Reject when
- the user wants the photo shown unchanged with no interpretation
- the source is already an illustration or graphic artwork
- the poster must reproduce a face with strict documentary fidelity
- no quiet space can be found in the composition

## Style DNA
**Subject / identity behavior**  
Keep the photograph truthful as the factual anchor, then let a larger abstract illustration field reinterpret selected source elements. The two materials meet at a visible hand-torn fibrous edge. The photograph provides the facts; the illustration decides how they remain.

**Composition strategy**  
Vertical 3:5 canvas of warm aged paper. Allocate the photographic portion and the illustration field so the illustration affects a substantial part of the poster, not a timid peripheral doodle. Enlarge the illustration by scale and field extent, never by adding detail. Keep substantial quiet paper both inside and around the enlarged illustration. Build photography, illustration and the added hue on one shared source-derived contour, axis, rhythm or field.

**Lighting language**  
Do not restage the photograph. Flat, even, scan-like light across the whole sheet. No cinematic lighting, no depth of field, no cast shadows from the paper. The only depth cue is the torn boundary itself.

**Color logic**  
Preserve the photograph's natural color atmosphere and recognizable geometry. Add exactly one high-chroma hue that is source-derived and shares shapes with the illustration rather than floating as decoration. The hue must be chromatic structure, not an accent sticker: it must change balance, movement, figure-ground or meaning.

**Material / texture language**  
Warm cream aged paper with matte fibers, restrained speckles, weathering and flat print texture. The torn edge shows irregular contour, narrow exposed fibers, warm paper tone and natural variation - never a uniform frame, never clean digital clipping, never a sticker outline or curled-paper depth. Speckles and ghost marks stay subordinate to the torn boundary.

**Typography system**  
One restrained source-aware micro-text element, English by default, or Chinese or bilingual when the user supplied or requested it. A deliberate standalone word, keyword sequence of two to four scene-related words with one separator system, or a short phrase. If bilingual, one line clearly subordinate. Legible, paper-integrated, quiet, placed in genuine breathing room - never a caption or headline.

**Realism level**  
medium

**Transformation strength**  
high

## Execution contract
1. Treat the uploaded source photograph as the single factual and visual anchor.
2. Protect the scene-analysis identity anchors before stylization.
3. Produce one finished poster, not a collage, contact sheet, moodboard, scrapbook, album, or multi-panel layout.
4. Preserve source truth: do not invent people, brands, dates, landmarks, signatures, seals, costumes, or factual claims.
5. Apply this style through its actual visual mechanism, not a generic preset.
6. Keep typography subordinate to the image, normally no more than 5–10% of the visual area.
7. Use the source-derived palette unless the style contract explicitly permits a controlled reinterpretation.
8. Stop stylization before it damages identity, perspective, material plausibility, or cultural accuracy.

## Style-specific prompt block
Transform the single source photograph using the **Gathered Scenes Zine** visual system. Preserve the signature: truthful photograph as anchor, illustration as field, color as structure, torn paper as boundary, paper that breathes.

Canvas: vertical 3:5 on warm cream aged paper with matte fibers, restrained speckles and weathering. Flat scan lighting, no mockup framing, no cast shadow.

Scene reading: identify the 1-2 core subjects, 2-3 supporting elements, the spatial invariants that must stay recognizable (horizon, relative positions, scale, overlap, facing direction), the dominant gesture, the visual-weight map, the native color atmosphere, one or two source shapes that can continue across photo and illustration, the natural quiet areas, and the semantic minimum - the smallest set of forms that still identifies this particular place.

Abstraction: compress dense foliage, branches, leaves, crowds, texture and other micro-detail into a few large quiet forms. Remove roughly 60-80% of small descriptive detail. Merge repeated elements into one rhythm or silhouette. Keep at least one unmistakable source-specific feature. Choose ONE primary illustration grammar - silhouette-led for figures and trees, contour-led for architecture and paths, field-led for water, fog and sky, rhythm-led for posts, windows, waves and steps, cut-paper-led when the source has a strong shape hierarchy - plus at most one supporting grammar. Never combine every print process in one illustration.

Allocation: the illustration must occupy a substantial part of the poster, enlarged by scale and field extent rather than by adding detail. Keep substantial internal and surrounding negative space so blank paper clarifies the forms instead of feeling accidentally empty. Let photography, illustration and color share one source-derived contour, axis, rhythm or field.

Torn-paper handoff: at the main photo-to-paper boundary, a visibly hand-torn fibrous edge - irregular contour, narrow exposed fibers, warm paper tone, natural variation, no uniform frame. Let zero to two selected forms cross the boundary when continuation helps. Speckles and ghost marks stay subordinate, confined to meaningful pressure points and faint at thumbnail size.

Color: preserve the photograph's natural color atmosphere. Add exactly ONE high-chroma hue that shares source-derived shapes with the illustration - it must be chromatic structure, not decoration. If the source carries a meaningful repeatable motif (flowers, leaves, small lights, windows, stones), you may disperse several instances of it in that single hue with unequal scale and interval, leaving some intervals open.

Typography: one restrained micro-text element. English by default; Chinese or bilingual only when supplied or requested. A deliberate standalone word, a two-to-four-word keyword sequence with one consistent separator, or a short phrase. Legible, paper-integrated, quiet, subordinate to the image, placed in genuine breathing room - never a caption or a headline. Never invent Chinese characters.

## Style-specific negative prompt
No photographic pixels replaced by illustration, no traced or rotoscoped full scene, no vintage texture laid over an intact photo, no generic mood labels without visible structure, no decorative metaphors, no sticker-like cutout outlines, no fuzzy selection halos, no clean digital clipping, no uniform torn border, no heavy cast shadows, no curled paper, no 3D depth, no scattered confetti, no arbitrary dots or grids, no tape, no multiple competing bright hues, no commercial advertising hierarchy, no logo, no CTA, no glossy mockup, no watermark, no photorealistic regions.

## Hybrid behavior
Direct styles may enter `Controlled Hybrid` only if their pair appears in `data/compatibility.json`. The primary keeps subject identity, composition, lighting, palette, and realism. The secondary controls one declared subsystem only.

## Common failure modes
- Applying the same texture or effect uniformly to every pixel.
- Replacing source-specific structure with generic AI imagery.
- Oversizing typography until text becomes the subject.
- Inventing content to make a style stereotype more obvious.
- Ignoring material differences between surfaces.
- Turning batch consistency into forced visual sameness.

## Self-check
- Is the source still immediately recognizable?
- Can major stylized elements be traced to the source or this style's mechanism?
- Is the composition materially different from a simple filter?
- Is text small, legible, and outside identity-critical regions?
- Are there invented facts, extra subjects, malformed structures, or fake characters?
- Does the output read as this style specifically rather than generic “AI art”?

## Acceptance criteria
Use `references/visual-benchmark.md`. A production image should normally score at least 17/20 across identity, composition, lighting, palette, material behavior, edges, typography, source specificity, style specificity, and anti-slop quality.
